19 · Sargam

The film-song cassette and its J-card, played on a two-in-one. For people who want their money with some 1980s swagger — bold stripes, wide lettering, warm colour — and the month shown as tape running from one reel to the other.

ShapeCassette geometry: 11px-radius label panels with a thin double-line frame, a recessed well for the tab strip, and chunky transport keys — a 4px bottom edge and one top highlight line, dropping 2px when pressed or held.
TypeMichroma for titles, section legends, keypad digits and the hero figure (54px on phone, 68px on desktop); Lexend 400–600 with tabular numerals for every row, amount and caption. The rupee sign is always set in Lexend.
DensityMedium with a generous hero: the label area takes about 40% of the Home screen; accounts sit as three label tiles so Latest still fits above the keys.
MotionOn load both hubs of the tape window turn a third of a revolution and settle (1.4s ease-out), then idle at one turn a minute unless reduced motion is set. Keys drop 2px on press. Nothing else moves.
VoicePlain. The only tape idiom is the reel caption, and it is the calendar: “16 days left”, “Day 15 of 31”, “15 days gone”. Product labels stay as they are.
SignatureThe tape window: the supply reel is the 16 days left, the take-up reel the 15 days gone, drawn to scale by area inside a ring that marks a full 31-day reel. Budgets are 12-bar level meters — teal to 70%, mustard to 90%, red beyond.
