8 · Ganak

The one who counts. The desk calculator every shopkeeper and parent kept by the till — putty plastic, rubber keys, a liquid-crystal window — for people who trust a figure more when it looks like it was punched in.

ShapeMoulded plastic. Panels are recessed bezels, never flat cards; buttons are rounded keys with a top highlight and a bottom lip that collapses when pressed.
TypeThe hero and the amount are seven-segment digits drawn as SVG, slanted, with unlit ghost segments behind. Everything else is Archivo with tabular numerals; key legends bold and wide.
DensityMedium. Content is grouped into key zones — display, memory, keypad — the way a calculator groups its buttons.
MotionPower-on test: the LCD flashes 88,88,888 for a beat, then settles to the figure. Keys sink on press; the budget at its limit blinks its last segment.
VoiceTerse legends only where they are true: M for the budget held in memory, GT beside the grand total. Every product label stays as written.
SignatureThe display window — LCD by day, glowing VFD on the black model — with status annunciators along its top edge and budgets drawn as bar-graph segments.
